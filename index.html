AEP Readiness Program

How we coordinated the cross-functional effort — and what stakeholders and teams said about it.

About this feedback

The feedback and data on this page comes from four workstream retro sessions held in December 2025, two post-AEP surveys, and a compiled summary deck. The full AEP Readiness hub from last year is available in Notion →

What we did

The AEP Readiness Program was the cross-functional coordination layer that ran from early August through December 7, 2025. It provided structure across four workstreams — External Communications, Product Release, and Internal Training, plus program-level oversight — to align on priorities, surface blockers, and move decisions forward. The season wrapped with a series of workstream-specific retro sessions in December.

Strong across certification and compliance topics

Agents rated AHIP/certifications and compliance highest of all topics — areas where Spark's readiness efforts were most structured and had the clearest timelines.

Non-commissionable plans were the biggest knowledge gap

With the lowest score of any topic (3.79/5), non-comm plans and plan exits were where agents felt least prepared — a complex, high-stakes area that required more proactive education.

Overall preparedness was solid given the season's chaos

3.97/5 and 77% finding training resources effective is a meaningful result given how turbulent AEP 2026 was — carrier changes, plan exits, and new processes all landing mid-season.

Agents wanted more hands-on, scenario-based training

The top ask was better training format — real client scenarios and demos rather than fast-moving slide decks. Agents also flagged wanting direct answers from support rather than being pointed to training videos.

What worked & what didn't

The framework held under pressure

Despite a tumultuous AEP, the program structure helped the company stay functional. 20+ survey respondents reported feeling prepared going into AEP.

We started earlier — but still not early enough

The August start created a reactive, fire-drill pace. Multiple stakeholders felt planning should start in Q2 or immediately following the prior AEP.

More coordinated than the prior year

Stakeholders noted improved visibility into ownership and clearer cross-functional workflows compared to the prior AEP.

No true single source of truth

Info lived across Slack, Notion, email, and the Help Center — making it easy to miss critical updates and hard to know where to look.

One point of contact made a real difference

Having a dedicated program lead to triage questions, translate complex carrier changes, and provide historical context was critical — especially with many new hires.

Ownership gaps created confusion

It was often unclear who owned key decisions once things kicked off. Webinars and suppressions were specifically called out as unclear.

New hires were set up well

The program helped onboarded employees understand the importance and challenges of AEP — a genuine success called out across multiple respondents.

Too much weight on one person

The program relied on a single point of contact in a way that wasn't sustainable. It worked as a workaround, not a scalable solution.

One-to-many channels unlocked more reach

Newsletters and webinars proved more effective than the high-touch, one-on-one interactions of prior years.

Account Management was underrepresented

AM's volume during AEP made it hard to participate in stakeholder work — and their feedback reflected it, with AM reporting the lowest preparedness of any team.

The AEP calendar was a hit

Multiple respondents called it out as a concrete improvement — keeping key dates and meetings visible to all stakeholders throughout the season.

Teams felt they couldn't push back

The program didn't create enough space to manage scope or decline requests, contributing to feeling under-resourced and reactive.

Newsletters and needs assessments appreciated

Agents mentioned newsletters and outreach tools as helpful for client communication and staying organized. The needs assessment workflow was specifically called out as a time saver.

Webinars rated highest of all external channels

Agents who engaged with training webinars gave them the highest ratings of any external resource — reinforcing the internal finding that webinars were the breakout channel of the season.

Booklet impact remains unclear

The booklet scored the lowest of all resources among agents (3.74/5) — consistent with the internal team finding where 69% of stakeholders said they weren't sure if it was helpful.

Agents want more reinforcement, not just more content

Multiple agents noted that emails and updates go unread. The ask wasn't more communication — it was more active reinforcement of key messages through multiple channels.

What worked & what didn't

Newsletter performance was strong

43% open rate and ~12% CTR are well above industry benchmarks — agents were reading it, and teams noticed fewer confusion-driven support calls as a result.

The process was not sustainable

Marketing (Erin, Fiona, and one copywriter) was overwhelmed by volume. The team never got more than 2–3 days ahead of sends — far short of the planned 1–2 week window.

Webinars became a breakout channel

Rapid-response webinars on non-commissionable plans, HRAs, and plan exits were the highest-rated external channel — a clear win that validated webinars as a core tool.

Reactive, not strategic

Comms were driven by what came in rather than a proactive plan. Too many one-off sends that could have been batched into the newsletter, adding to marketing's load.

Marketing brief request streamlined intake

Standardizing how teams submitted comms requests made the process cleaner — called out as a genuine improvement over the prior year's ad hoc approach.

Decision-making bottleneck created swirl

Many teams had input on what got communicated, but approvals funneled through a narrow group of senior stakeholders — causing delays and internal confusion for marketing.

SMEs distilled complex info well

Subject matter experts across the business were effective at translating carrier updates and product changes into content marketing could actually use.

No single source of truth for support teams

Britney and Asheley's teams couldn't easily find what had been sent to agents. Agents called in about comms support had no context for — or found outdated versions.

SMS and new channels validated

Successfully tested SMS to agency principals and expanded beyond email-only. These channels are now proven and ready to scale for 2027.

Uncoordinated webinar strategy

No single owner managed the webinar calendar. Conflicting schedules and reactive overuse led to agent fatigue and webinars cannibalizing each other's attendance.

Proactive comms reduced support noise

Agents who received consistent weekly updates came into the support inbox better informed — a direct quality-of-life improvement for Tier 1 and PSS teams.

Newsletter became too long

Ad hoc requests from leadership kept bloating the content, diluting focus. Agents received too much information at once with no clear prioritization.

Platform impact was a standout strength

4.51/5 with 89% rating it 4 or higher — agents overwhelmingly felt the Spark platform made a meaningful difference to their business this AEP. New features like retention campaigns, plan exit alerts, and cheat sheets were consistently well-rated.

Needs assessments and cheat sheets were agent favorites

Agents frequently called out needs assessments as a time saver and cheat sheets as a useful reference tool — direct validation of two of the season's key feature releases.

Sunfire reliability undermined the agent experience

Sunfire was the single most complained-about pain point in the agent survey — inaccurate drug and prescription data, missing plans, unreliable provider lookups, and plans not available for enrollment. This wasn't a Spark release failure, but it significantly affected how agents experienced the season.

AI Assistant feedback was more mixed

The AI Assistant scored lower (~3.9/5) than other features — suggesting agents found it useful but not yet essential. Likely a function of awareness and adoption rather than capability.

What worked & what didn't

Delivered an ambitious roadmap

All major features shipped before Oct 15 — recognized internally as a significant achievement given the scope and team size going into one of Spark's biggest AEPs.

Strategy finalized too late

AEP product strategy wasn't locked until mid-July. The team went from "we have time" to "we don't have time" quickly, compressing everything that followed.

Cross-functional coordination improved

Jon's central role created shared accountability across product, marketing, and comms — teams felt more informed and aligned than in prior years.

Compressed timeline created team strain

The final push was six intense weeks with significant stress across engineering, product, and marketing — compounded by competing priorities like new hires and the LOA platform transition running in parallel.

Plan Cheat Sheets drove measurable lead gen

1,000 leads with 500 net new was a first for Spark — unlocked by feature ungating and a more strategic marketing approach built around the product.

Some releases shipped with bugs

Fixed marketing deadlines meant UAT time was cut to hit dates, resulting in some features going live before they were fully ready.

Product webinars proved effective

The AI Assistant deep dive and AEP kickoff webinar gave agents a level of fidelity that written guides alone couldn't achieve — validating webinars as a product education format.

Release date tension between product and marketing

Marketing needed committed dates early for planning; product couldn't always hold those dates due to CMS timelines and engineering realities. Both sides had legitimate needs with no clean resolution.

Narrative shift landed well

Framing Spark as a tech leader — rather than a feature list — was a genuine step forward. The AI Assistant story gave the team something compelling to rally around.

Releases came too close to AEP for some teams

Teams didn't have enough runway to educate agency principals on new features before AEP started, limiting adoption and creating confusion during the season.

Feature ungating explored for the first time

Looping in the right stakeholders early enough to explore ungating opened up a new growth channel — one that will be built on in future AEPs.

Documentation was scattered

Information lived across multiple redundant docs — handoff decks, release calendars, guides — creating confusion and version control issues across teams.

What worked & what didn't

T1 and Product Support training was effective

Teams that received targeted training came out of AEP noticeably more confident — reduced cross-functional questions and faster issue resolution showed up across the feedback.

AM training was a clear gap

Account Managers felt unprepared for agent questions — particularly around contracting and Sunfire. AM was the only team to rate training effectiveness at 1/5.

Weekly office hours were highly valuable pre-AEP

The troubleshooting format worked especially well in the weeks before and at the start of AEP — when volume was manageable and questions were fresh.

Training was reactive, not proactive

Content was built to solve immediate problems as they surfaced — not from a structured, phased curriculum built well in advance of the season.

Internal product release training landed well

Sessions on the AI Assistant and AEP Retention Campaign before they went live gave T1 and Product Support the context they needed to support agents effectively.

Resources were scattered

Critical information lived across Slack threads, Notion pages, and individual docs — managers had to build their own ad hoc reference hubs to fill the gaps.

Asking teams what they wanted to learn was a positive step

Soliciting team input before building the training program helped sessions address real gaps, not just assumed ones — a practice worth scaling next year.

No sandbox environment for Tier 1 / Product Support

Teams had to learn Spark and Sunfire navigation live with agents — no practice environment existed to build confidence before calls started coming in.

New hires understood AEP's importance

The all-hands kickoff and onboarding training successfully brought new employees up to speed on what AEP meant for their role — called out as a genuine win.

Training timing was often misaligned

When training happened too far in advance of when teams needed the knowledge, retention suffered — particularly for new hires still ramping up.

AEP calendar provided training visibility

Teams knew when sessions were happening, which improved attendance and allowed managers to plan their team's preparation schedule.

Cross-functional training was minimal

Teams largely trained in silos. Requests for shadowing and cross-team learning were common in the feedback but weren't built into the program.

What worked & what didn't

Brian Wu's enrollment tool caught real issues

Compliance was not a planned workstream

Key themes

Most agents felt prepared for contracting & RTS

77% of agents rated themselves prepared or better for Contracting/RTS heading into AEP — the strongest preparedness score of any topic in the agent survey. Agents who engaged early with carrier certification timelines generally arrived RTS without incident.

Deadlines, transfers, and blackouts were well-managed

The Contracting team specifically identified deadline tracking, transfer processing, and carrier blackout navigation as areas they felt well-prepared for. Pre-AEP Anthem outreach and structured carrier deadline communications helped agents move through transfer windows before cutoffs.

RTS lag was a persistent friction point

Multiple agents reported that carrier portals were showing RTS status before Spark reflected it — creating confusion at the start of AEP. The disconnect between carrier-confirmed RTS and the Spark platform's RTS display generated support volume and frustrated agents trying to quote and enroll.

RTS reporting and add-state processes were a moving target

The Contracting team flagged this as their primary gap: "all things having to do with the moving target that is ready to sell reporting from carriers and add state processes." This was echoed by ACS and Product stakeholders, who noted last-minute discoveries around SNP certs, state licensing requirements, and add-state workflows that weren't surfaced until right before or during AEP.

Humana blackout had real sales impact

Agents who weren't contracted with Humana before the blackout window lost significant opportunities — one agent cited approximately 30 lost sales. New agents entering AEP without all key carriers contracted was a recurring complaint, pointing to a gap in proactive pre-AEP contracting outreach.

Contracting UI change frustrated agents mid-prep

A platform update to the carrier contracting page was called out unprompted across multiple agent responses: agents found it harder to use than in 2024 and 2025, and could no longer click directly into a carrier to request a contract. The change landed close enough to AEP to create friction at a critical moment.

Proactive transfer deadline reminders were insufficient

Marielle self-identified this gap directly: "I could have done a better job with deadline prep and reminders for agents in advance — especially for transfers with a release." Agents with pending transfer forms approaching blackout windows didn't always receive timely reminders, resulting in missed deadlines.

For 2027

Start contracting outreach in June

Both the Contracting team and agents recommended starting earlier — at or before the time AHIP/carrier certs begin. Early outreach prevents the bottleneck of agents entering AEP without all contracts in place.

Structured transfer deadline reminder cadence

Build and own a proactive reminder schedule for contract deadlines, especially transfers with a release. Agents and uplines need targeted prompts well before blackout windows close — not just general deadline communications.

Proactive hierarchy audit before AEP

Run a hierarchy and level audit earlier in the cycle to catch misalignments before they create contracting delays during the season. Flag stragglers by carrier and escalate to uplines proactively.

Get uplines involved earlier and more explicitly

Uplines need clearer asks and earlier involvement in pushing their downlines across the contracting finish line. Agents who arrive at AEP without key carriers are often there because their upline wasn't engaged soon enough.

No major contracting platform changes in Q4

Allison's explicit 2027 ask: no major internal or external system changes in the last quarter heading into deadline season. Changes to the contracting UI or workflows mid-prep create avoidable support volume at the worst possible time.

"Ready to Sell: what to expect" agent guide

Allison flagged this as a missing topic: agents needed clearer guidance on what the RTS process actually looks like — timelines, what triggers it, and how to troubleshoot lag between carrier confirmation and Spark reflection.

For 2027

Pre-AEP contracting readiness audit by agency

Run a proactive audit before AEP identifying which agents in each agency's downline aren't yet RTS. Share that list with APs and do personalized outreach to stragglers — don't wait for agents to surface the problem mid-season.

Dedicated AM training on contracting and Sunfire

AM teams need baseline competency in contracting processes and Sunfire troubleshooting before AEP starts — not just product feature training. They are the first point of contact for APs; they need to be able to answer or triage without escalating everything.

Contracting cutoff dates for new onboardings

Establish a cutoff date for onboarding new agencies before AEP. Handling first-time contracting and onboarding during peak AEP volume is a known strain on both AM and the Contracting team. A hard cutoff would shift that work into a more manageable window.

Centralized source of truth — Notion, not Slack

AEP updates, channel guides, carrier changes, and process docs should live in one structured place. Multiple respondents independently arrived at Notion as the answer. Slack threads work for real-time updates but fail as a reference source, especially for field teams on the road.

Earlier product releases and agency education lead time

New platform features need to be in agencies' hands with enough lead time for AMs to actually teach them. The ask is consistent: get releases out earlier so AM can spend AEP answering questions, not explaining tools they just received themselves.

Separate planning track for telephonic agencies

Field agencies and call center agencies have different workflows, tools, and needs. As Spark onboards more telephonic partners, AEP readiness planning should account for both — including Sunfire Blaze, voice signature compliance, and call center-specific Spark workflows.

Push for RTS earlier — when certs open

A bigger, more proactive push to get agents contracted and RTS as soon as AHIP and carrier certs are available would reduce the end-of-season contracting bottleneck that burdened both AM and the Contracting team this cycle.

More thought leadership content for agency principals

Sales wants more substance to bring to AP relationships: carrier strategy, HRA, ancillary product stacks, and lead generation. More regular webinars and thought leadership positioning would strengthen the AM/Sales value proposition beyond operational support.
